A few things are worth sorting early, especially your passport, which can take weeks to replace if there's a problem.

Before you book
1

Check your passport now

Your passport needs more than six months left before it expires, and it can't be damaged. A torn or water-marked passport will be refused at the border.

Check it today, not in May 2027. Renewals take time.

2

Apply for your Visa on Arrival

Do this online before you fly. It's about A$45 per person, valid for 30 days. Apply at evisa.imigrasi.go.id.

Australian passport holders: once this is done online you can walk straight through the e-gates on arrival. No queueing at the visa desk.
3

Complete your All Indonesia Arrival Card

Free, and completed within 72 hours of flying, not before. You'll get a QR code; save it to your phone or print it.

4

Pay the Bali tourist levy

Every visitor pays a tourist fee of roughly A$14. Pay before you go, or on arrival if you forget.

lovebali.baliprov.go.id

Worth having. If you get sick you can claim the doctor's visit back.

The practical stuff: what to pack, how to get around, what things cost, and where to eat.

Good to know

Set these up before you fly

Gojek is Bali's version of Uber, and how you'll get everywhere. It also does food delivery. Download it and add your card before you leave; you can also pay cash.

An eSIM is much easier than hunting for a local SIM. Holafly and Simify both work well.

Worth packing

  • Hand sanitiser
  • Gastro-stop, not because you'll definitely get sick, but a change in food and climate can upset your stomach
  • Sunscreen, always more expensive overseas
  • Hydralyte for day to day

Pocari Sweat is the local electrolyte drink, and any convenience store or supermarket has it.

If you get sick

Dr Ari is who we'd call. Keep your receipts. Travel insurance will usually reimburse you.

You don't need any vaccinations or medication to travel to Bali. Just make sure your standard tetanus and diphtheria shots are current.

Weather in June

It's dry season: warm and dry through the day, cooler in the evening but never cold.

The sun sets around 6:30pm and it turns comfortable. Light trousers are plenty. You won't need a jacket.

Cards and cash

You can pay by card almost everywhere. We don't bother bringing cash, we just withdraw rupiah from an ATM once we're there.

You can also change money easily at the airport when you land.

Tipping

It's customary to tip for service in Bali.
